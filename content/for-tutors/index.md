## The shape of a session

A typical hour:

| Minutes | What happens | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | **Warm-up recall.** Three quick questions from last time, no notes. | Retrieval is the strongest form of practice, and it shows you what stuck. |
| 5–15 | **Find the real gap.** Ask the student to explain a problem out loud. Listen for the first step where the reasoning goes wrong. | The homework question is rarely the actual problem. The gap is usually one or two lessons earlier. |
| 15–35 | **Teach one idea.** Puzzle first, then the common wrong idea, then the explanation, then the sim. | One idea taught well beats three skimmed. |
| 35–50 | **Worked example, then their turn.** Do one fully, then hand over a half-done one, then a fresh one. | Fading the help builds independence without leaving them stranded. |
| 50–60 | **Wrap-up.** They explain the idea back to you in their own words, and you agree what to practise. | If they can teach it, they know it. |

## Habits that matter more than any explanation

- **Ask, then wait.** Count to five silently after a question. Most students answer if you give them the time.
- **Have them predict before the sim runs.** "Which way will the line tilt?" A wrong prediction followed by the right result is the most memorable moment in a lesson.
- **Name misconceptions without judgement.** "Most people think this at first, and here's why it feels right."
- **Draw everything.** A diagram with the knowns labelled solves half of any physics problem.
- **Check units and signs out loud.** Make it a habit they hear you do.
- **End on something they got right.**

## Diagnosing where to start

Use the **prerequisites** on each lesson. If a student is stuck on projectile motion, check acceleration; if that is shaky, check velocity as a slope. The [topic map](../map/index.md) shows these chains.

## Using the sims live

Share the sim on screen or hand over the mouse. Give the student control of the sliders as soon as possible. Your job is to ask the questions in **Try this**, not to drive.
