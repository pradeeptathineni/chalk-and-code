I have tutored math from early grade school through middle school. The aim is always the same: see *why* a rule works before memorising it. Each unit comes with something to drag, slide or play with.

| # | Unit | What it covers | Interactive | Grades | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Number sense | place value, mental math, estimating, multiplication as area | base-ten blocks you can break apart | K–4 | Planned |
| 2 | Fractions | what a fraction is, equivalent fractions, adding and multiplying them | fraction bars and pizzas | 3–6 | Planned |
| 3 | Decimals and percents | tenths and hundredths, percent as "out of 100", discounts and tips | hundred grid | 4–7 | Planned |
| 4 | Negative numbers | the number line, temperature and debt, adding and subtracting negatives | walk the number line | 6–7 | Planned |
| 5 | Ratios and rates | ratio tables, unit rates, speed as a rate, scaling recipes and maps | double number line | 6–7 | Planned |
| 6 | Expressions and equations | variables, balancing equations, solving for x | balance scale | 6–8 | Planned |
| 7 | Geometry | area, perimeter, volume, angles, the Pythagorean theorem | shapes you can stretch | 4–8 | Planned |
| 8 | Graphs and functions | the coordinate plane, plotting points, lines and slope | slider-driven line plotter | 6–8 | Planned |
| 9 | Data and chance | averages, charts, probability with coins and dice | thousands of dice rolled at once | 4–8 | Planned |

Units 5 and 8 lead straight into [Physics: Motion](../physics/01-motion/index.md), where speed is a rate and the slope of a graph is a velocity.
