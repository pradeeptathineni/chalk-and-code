## Start here

On a cold morning, a metal bench feels much colder than a wooden one right next to it. They've been sitting in the same air all night. Is the metal really colder?

## The common wrong idea

"Cold is a thing that flows into you."

There's no such thing as "cold" flowing anywhere. **Heat** flows, always from **hotter to colder**. The metal bench and the wooden one are at the same temperature. The metal just carries heat away from your hand much faster, so your hand cools quickly and feels cold.

## The idea

**Temperature is jiggling.** The temperature of something is a measure of how fast its particles are jiggling on average. Hotter means faster.

**There's a coldest possible temperature.** If you slow the particles down as much as possible, you reach **absolute zero**: −273 °C. Nothing can get colder, because the particles can't jiggle any less.

**Heat is energy on the move** from hot things to cold things. It moves three ways:

- **Conduction (touching):** fast-jiggling particles bump into their neighbours and pass the jiggle along. A metal spoon in hot soup gets hot all the way up the handle. Metals conduct heat very well; wood, plastic, air and wool conduct it badly, which is why they keep you warm.
- **Convection (flowing):** hot liquids and gases spread out, become lighter and rise, while cooler ones sink. That flow carries heat around a pot of soup, a room, or the whole atmosphere.
- **Radiation (glowing):** everything gives off infrared light, and hotter things give off more. That's how the Sun warms you across 150 million km of empty space, and how you feel the warmth of a campfire on your face.

## Worked example

Why does a wool sweater keep you warm, even though it doesn't make any heat?

1. Your body makes heat, and heat flows from hot (you) to cold (the air around you).
2. Wool is full of tiny pockets of trapped air, and still air conducts heat very badly.
3. So the heat leaks away from you **much more slowly**. The sweater doesn't add warmth; it keeps yours in.

## Your turn (faded example)

You hold a cold can of soda.

1. Which way does heat flow: from you to the can, or the can to you? ___
2. So your hand gets ___ and the can gets ___.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Heat flows from your hand (hotter) to the can (colder). Your hand gets cooler and the can gets slightly warmer. No "cold" moved at all.

</details>

## Practice

1. Why do pans often have plastic or wooden handles?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Plastic and wood conduct heat badly, so the handle stays cool enough to hold while the metal pan conducts heat well into the food.

</details>

2. Why is the top floor of a house often the warmest?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Convection: warm air is lighter than cool air, so it rises and collects at the top of the house.

</details>
