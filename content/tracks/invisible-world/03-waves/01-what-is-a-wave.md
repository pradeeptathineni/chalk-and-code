> **After this lesson you can:**
> - explain what a wave carries, and what it doesn't
> - tell a side-to-side (transverse) wave from a push-and-pull (longitudinal) wave

## Start here

A rubber duck floats in a pond. You drop a stone in the middle and ripples spread out to the duck. Does the duck get carried to the edge of the pond? What does it do instead?

## The common wrong idea

"A wave carries water (or rope, or air) along with it."

The duck just bobs up and down as the ripples pass. It stays in about the same place. A wave carries **energy** from one place to another, but the water itself mostly stays where it was. Think of a stadium wave: the wave races round the stadium, but every fan stays in their seat, just standing up and sitting down.

## The idea

A **wave** is a disturbance that travels, carrying energy without carrying the material along.

There are two kinds:

- **Transverse waves**: the material moves **side to side** (or up and down) while the wave moves along. A rope you shake, ripples on water, a stadium wave. Light is a transverse wave too, but it doesn't need any material at all, which is why it can cross empty space.
- **Longitudinal waves**: the material moves **back and forth**, in the same direction the wave travels. Push the end of a slinky and a squashed-up bit runs along it. **Sound** is a longitudinal wave: squashed and stretched air.

## Worked example

Is a wave of falling dominoes a wave in the physics sense?

1. Does a disturbance travel along? Yes, the falling moves down the line.
2. Does energy travel? Yes, each domino passes energy to the next.
3. Does each domino stay roughly where it was? Yes, it just tips over in place.
4. So it **behaves like a wave** (though, unlike a real wave, it can only happen once: the dominoes don't stand back up).

## Your turn (faded example)

You and a friend hold a slinky stretched along the floor. You push your end forward once.

1. Which way do the coils move? ___
2. Which way does the squashed-up bit travel? ___
3. So it's a ___ wave.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The coils move back and forth along the slinky, the same direction the squashed bit travels. That makes it a longitudinal wave, like sound.

</details>

## Practice

1. A surfer rides a wave all the way to the beach. Doesn't that prove waves carry stuff along?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The surfer is sliding down the front slope of the wave, a bit like sliding down a moving hill, using the wave's energy. The water itself mostly goes round in circles in place. (Near the beach, where waves break, water does tumble forward, but that's the wave falling apart.)

</details>

2. Is sound transverse or longitudinal?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Longitudinal. The air is pushed and pulled back and forth in the same direction the sound travels.

</details>
