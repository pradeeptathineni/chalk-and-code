> **After this lesson you can:**
> - point to the amplitude and the wavelength of a wave
> - explain what frequency means and measure it
> - use speed = wavelength × frequency

## Start here

You shake a rope faster and faster. Do the waves get longer or shorter? Make a guess, then try it in the sim.

## The common wrong idea

"Shaking faster makes the wave travel faster."

On the same rope, pulled just as tight, waves always travel at the **same speed**. Shaking faster makes **more** waves, so each one has to be **shorter** to fit. The speed depends on the rope (how heavy, how tight), not on your hand.

## The idea

Four numbers describe a steady wave:

- **Amplitude**: how far the rope moves from its resting line. Bigger amplitude means more energy: taller water waves, louder sounds, brighter light.
- **Wavelength**: the length of one whole wave, crest to crest.
- **Frequency**: how many waves pass by each second, in **hertz** (Hz). It's also how many times per second your hand shakes.
- **Speed**: how far a crest travels each second.

They're linked by one rule:

1204\text{speed} = \text{wavelength} \times \text{frequency}1204

Why? In one second, *frequency* waves go past, and each is *wavelength* long, so the total distance the wave moves in a second is wavelength × frequency.

## Worked example

Waves travel along a rope at 3 m/s and you shake it 1.5 times a second. How long is each wave?

1. speed = wavelength × frequency, so wavelength = speed ÷ frequency.
2.  \div 1.5 = 2$.
3. Each wave is **2 m** long. (Set the sim to 3 m/s and 1.5 Hz to check.)

## Your turn (faded example)

Water waves 4 m long pass a pier, and 0.5 of a wave passes every second.

1. speed = wavelength × frequency = ___ × ___
2. speed = ___

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

 \times 0.5 = 2$ m/s.

</details>

## Practice

1. Sound travels through air at about 340 m/s. A piano's middle C has a frequency of about 262 Hz. Roughly how long is the sound wave?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

40 \div 262 \approx 1.3$ m. Each sound wave from middle C is about as long as a 9-year-old is tall.

</details>

2. You double the frequency on the same rope. What happens to the wavelength?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

It halves. The speed stays the same, so if frequency doubles, wavelength must halve to keep wavelength × frequency the same.

</details>
