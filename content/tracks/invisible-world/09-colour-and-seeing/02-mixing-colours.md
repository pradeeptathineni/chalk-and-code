## Start here

In art class, you learn that mixing all your paints together makes a muddy brown-black. But mix all the colours of light together and you get white. How can mixing colours make opposite results?

## The common wrong idea

"Mixing colours works the same way for paint and for light."

They work in opposite directions. **Light adds:** each new light adds more light, so mixing gets brighter, all the way to white. **Paint takes away:** each paint soaks up some of the light that hits it, so mixing soaks up more and more, getting darker, towards black.

## The idea

**Mixing light (adding).** Start in the dark and add lights:

| Mix | You see |
|---|---|
| red + green | yellow |
| green + blue | cyan (sky blue) |
| red + blue | magenta (pink-purple) |
| red + green + blue | white |

**Every screen works this way.** Look at a phone or TV screen through a magnifying glass, or put a tiny drop of water on it (on a phone only, and wipe it off after). You'll see it's made of millions of tiny red, green and blue dots called **pixels**. Turning each dot up or down mixes every colour you've ever seen on a screen. A white screen is all three at full brightness.

**Mixing paint (taking away).** Paint gets its colour by soaking up some colours of light and reflecting the rest. Printers use **cyan**, **magenta** and **yellow** inks:

- cyan soaks up red
- magenta soaks up green
- yellow soaks up blue

Mix cyan and yellow and between them they soak up red and blue, leaving only green. Mix all three and they soak up nearly everything: dark. (Printers add a separate black ink, because mixing all three makes a muddy brown rather than a true black.)

## Worked example

What colour do you get mixing magenta and yellow paint?

1. Magenta soaks up green. Yellow soaks up blue.
2. Together they soak up green and blue.
3. Only **red** is left to reflect.

## Practice

1. A pixel on your screen has its red and blue dots fully on and its green dot off. What colour is it?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Magenta: red light plus blue light.

</details>

2. Why don't printers use red, green and blue ink, like screens do?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Ink works by taking away. Red ink soaks up green and blue, so mixing red and green ink would soak up almost everything and make a dark mess. Cyan, magenta and yellow each take away just one of the three, so mixing them can make any colour.

</details>
