Colour feels like something objects simply have. It isn't: it's a conversation between light, objects and the three kinds of colour sensor in your eyes. One sim runs through the unit: [mixing colours](../../../sims/colour-mixing/index.html), with spotlights, paints, and coloured lamps shining on everyday things.

**Grades:** 4–8. All three lessons work from grade 4, and a flashlight with coloured cellophane makes a great live demo.

| # | Lesson | Level | Sim |

| 1 | [How we see: light, eyes and three colour sensors](./01-how-we-see.md) | 1 | mixing colours |
| 2 | [Mixing light and mixing paint](./02-mixing-colours.md) | 2 | mixing colours |
| 3 | [Why things have colour: red apples, blue skies and orange sunsets](./03-why-things-have-colour.md) | 2 | mixing colours |

**Hands on:** three flashlights with red, green and blue cellophane over them, shone on a white wall in a dark room. A magnifying glass on a screen. A glass of water with a few drops of milk and a flashlight shining through it: from the side it looks bluish, from the end it looks orange, like a tiny sky and sunset.

**Unit check:** if you can explain to someone why a red apple looks black under blue light and why the sky is blue, you have this unit at level 4.
