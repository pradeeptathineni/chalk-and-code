> **After this lesson you can:**
> - explain why an object looks the colour it does
> - predict what colour something will look under coloured light
> - explain why the sky is blue and sunsets are red

## Start here

Is a red apple red in the dark? Is it red under a blue lamp? What does "red" even mean if it depends on the light?

## The common wrong idea

"Colour is painted onto things. A red apple is red, whatever the light."

An apple doesn't *have* red the way it has a stalk. It **reflects** red light and **soaks up** the other colours. In white light, which contains all colours, the red bounces back to your eyes. Under pure blue light there's no red to reflect, so the apple soaks up everything and looks **black**.

## The idea

- **White** things reflect all colours. **Black** things soak up all colours (which is why a black T-shirt gets hot in the sun: the light it soaks up turns into heat).
- A **coloured** thing reflects its own colour and soaks up the rest.
- So what colour something *looks* depends on two things: what light shines on it, and what it reflects.

**Why is the sky blue?** Sunlight is white, a mix of all colours. On its way through the air, it bumps into air molecules (the tiny particles air is made of), and these scatter **blue** light (short waves) all over the sky much more than red light (long waves). Look anywhere in the sky and you see that scattered blue.

**Why are sunsets red and orange?** At sunset, sunlight travels through much more air to reach you. By the time it arrives, most of the blue has been scattered away sideways, leaving the reds and oranges.

And in space, with no air to scatter light, the sky is black even when the Sun is shining. Astronauts on the Moon saw a pitch-black sky in full daylight.

## Worked example

What does a green leaf look like under red light?

1. A leaf reflects green light and soaks up red and blue.
2. Red light shines on it, and it soaks that up.
3. Nothing reflects, so the leaf looks **black** (or very dark).

## Your turn (faded example)

What does a white T-shirt look like under green light?

1. White reflects ___ colours.
2. The only light arriving is ___.
3. So the T-shirt looks ___.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

White reflects all colours, but only green is arriving, so it reflects green and looks green.

</details>

## Practice

1. Why do people in hot, sunny places often wear white clothes?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

White reflects most of the sunlight, so less of it is soaked up and turned into heat. Black clothes soak up more light and get hotter.

</details>

2. A banana is under red light. What colour does it look?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Red. A banana reflects red and green; only red is arriving, so it reflects red.

</details>
