Electricity and magnetism look like separate subjects until you put a compass next to a wire. This unit follows the two great discoveries that joined them: Ørsted's twitching compass (current makes magnetism) and Faraday's moving magnet (magnetism makes current). Together they explain motors, speakers and almost every power station on Earth. One sim runs through the unit: [electromagnets and generators](../../../sims/electromagnet/index.html).

**Grades:** 5–8. Building a real electromagnet from a nail, some insulated wire and a battery is a classic, and works from grade 4 with an adult.

**Hands on:** a large iron nail, a metre of thin insulated wire, a battery and paperclips make a working electromagnet. Keep it connected only for a few seconds at a time: the wire gets warm.

**Unit check:** if you can explain to someone why a power station spins a magnet, and why a scrapyard crane can drop a car, you have this unit at level 4.
