## Start here

A scrapyard crane picks up a whole car with a giant magnet, swings it over a pile, and drops it. How does it let go? An ordinary magnet would hang on forever.

## The common wrong idea

"A stronger battery is the only way to make a stronger electromagnet."

More current helps, but so does **more wire**, and the biggest boost of all costs nothing: **put a piece of iron in the middle**.

## The idea

An **electromagnet** is a coil of wire with current flowing through it, usually wound around an iron core. Three things make it stronger:

1. **More turns of wire.** Each loop adds its field to the rest.
2. **More current.** More charge flowing makes a stronger field from every loop.
3. **An iron core.** Iron is full of tiny magnetic regions, normally pointing every which way. The coil's field lines them all up, and together they add a field far stronger than the coil's own: hundreds of times stronger in a real electromagnet.

And the best part: **switch off the current and the magnet switches off.** That's how the scrapyard crane drops the car. Electromagnets are everywhere: doorbells, door locks, speakers and headphones, electric motors, MRI scanners in hospitals, and the relays that let a small current switch a big one.

## Worked example

Strength in the sim is turns × current (× 40 with the iron core). A coil has 10 turns, carries 2 A and has an iron core. How strong is it?

1. Turns × current $= 10 \times 2 = 20$.
2. With the core, $\times 40$: 0 \times 40 = 800$.
3. Strength **800**.

## Your turn (faded example)

The same coil, 10 turns at 2 A, but without the core.

1. Turns × current $= \;?$
2. No core, so strength $= \;?$
3. How many times stronger was it with the core? ___

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

0 \times 2 = 20$, and with no core the strength is just 20. The core made it 40 times stronger.

</details>

## Practice

1. You want to double an electromagnet's strength but you can't change the battery. What could you do?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Double the number of turns of wire. (Or add an iron core if it doesn't have one, which does far more than double it.)

</details>

2. Why does a scrapyard use an electromagnet and not a giant ordinary magnet?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

So it can let go. Switch off the current and the electromagnet stops being a magnet, dropping the metal exactly where it's wanted.

</details>
