## Start here

You can sit by a sunny window all afternoon and not get sunburned. Sit outside in the same sun for the same time and you might. What's the window doing?

## The common wrong idea

"All radiation is dangerous."

"Radiation" just means energy spreading out from somewhere. The light from a lamp is radiation. So is the warmth from a campfire, and the radio signal in your car. What matters is **how much energy each packet of light carries**, and that depends on the wavelength. Only the short waves on the right of the spectrum, ultraviolet and beyond, carry enough to damage cells.

## The idea

**Ultraviolet** ("beyond violet") was found in 1801 by Johann Ritter, a year after infrared. He noticed that a chemical that darkens in light darkened fastest *past* the violet end of a rainbow, where nothing could be seen.

- Ultraviolet from the Sun has enough energy to damage skin cells. Your skin's response is a sunburn, and over many years, worse damage. Sunscreen, hats and shade block it.
- Ordinary window glass blocks most of the ultraviolet that burns. That's the window's secret.
- Some materials soak up ultraviolet and give the energy back as visible light. That's why posters and white T-shirts glow under a "black light".
- Bees can see ultraviolet. Many flowers have ultraviolet patterns, invisible to us, that guide bees to the nectar.

**X-rays** were found in 1895 by Wilhelm Röntgen. They have so much energy that they pass straight through skin and muscle, but bone stops more of them. Put a detector behind your hand and you get a shadow picture of your bones. Doctors keep X-ray doses small, and you wear a lead apron to protect the parts not being pictured.

**Gamma rays** have the shortest waves and the most energy of all. They come from radioactive atoms and from the most violent events in space, like exploding stars. Doctors aim them very carefully to destroy cancer cells.

## Worked example

Why does an X-ray picture show bones white and the rest of the hand dark?

1. X-rays pass through soft tissue (skin, muscle) easily.
2. Bone is denser and stops more of them.
3. The detector behind the hand turns *dark* where X-rays reach it.
4. Behind the bone, fewer X-rays arrive, so those parts stay **light**. The picture is a shadow of your bones.

## Your turn (faded example)

You lie on a beach under an umbrella and still get a little sunburned.

1. Does the umbrella block ultraviolet coming straight down from the Sun? ___
2. Sand and water reflect some ultraviolet. Where does that reflected light come from? ___
3. So where did the sunburn come from? ___

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The umbrella blocks the direct sunlight, but ultraviolet also bounces off the sand and water and reaches you from below and the sides. That's why you still need sunscreen in the shade at the beach.

</details>

## Practice

1. Put these in order of energy per packet, lowest first: X-ray, wifi, visible, ultraviolet.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Wifi, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray. Same order as shortest wavelength last.

</details>

2. Why does the dentist put a heavy apron on you before an X-ray?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The apron has lead in it, which stops X-rays. It protects the rest of your body, so only the small area being pictured gets a dose.

</details>
