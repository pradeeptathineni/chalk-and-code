## Start here

A car radio, a TV remote and a rainbow. One of them is not like the others... or is it? Make a guess before you read on: what could those three things possibly have in common?

## The common wrong idea

"Radio, light and the beam from a TV remote are completely different things."

They aren't. All three are **light**. They are all the same kind of wave, made of electricity and magnetism, travelling at the same enormous speed. The only thing that changes is how long each wave is. Our eyes happen to notice one small range of lengths, and we call that range "light". The rest is still light; we just can't see it.

## The idea

Shake one end of a skipping rope up and down and a wave runs along it. Two words describe that wave:

- **Wavelength**: the length of one complete wave, from one crest (top) to the next.
- **Frequency**: how many waves pass a point every second. We measure it in **hertz** (Hz). 1 Hz means one wave per second.

Here's the key fact about light: **every kind of light travels at the same speed**, about 300,000 km every second. That's fast enough to go round the Earth seven and a half times in one second.

So if the speed is fixed, long waves and short waves can't pass by equally often. Picture a line of long train carriages and a line of short ones, both moving at the same speed. More short carriages pass you every second. With waves:

$$\text{speed} = \text{wavelength} \times \text{frequency}$$

Shorter wavelength, higher frequency. Longer wavelength, lower frequency. Always.

## Worked example

A wave runs along a rope at 6 metres every second. Each wave is 2 metres long. How many waves pass your hand every second?

1. In one second, 6 m of wave goes past.
2. Each wave is 2 m long, so the number of waves is $6 \div 2 = 3$.
3. The frequency is **3 waves per second**, or 3 Hz.

## Your turn (faded example)

The same rope, still 6 m every second, but now you shake it faster and the waves are only 0.5 m long.

1. In one second, ___ m of wave goes past.
2. Number of waves $= 6 \div 0.5 = \;?$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

12 waves pass every second: 12 Hz. Shorter waves at the same speed means more of them each second.

</details>

## Practice

1. FM radio waves are about 3 m long. Wifi waves are about 12.5 cm long. Which has the higher frequency?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Wifi. Its waves are shorter, and all light travels at the same speed, so more wifi waves pass each second.

</details>

2. **Stretch (grades 7–8):** light travels at 300,000,000 m every second. An FM station sends waves 3 m long. What is its frequency?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$300{,}000{,}000 \div 3 = 100{,}000{,}000$ Hz, which is 100 million hertz or 100 MHz. That's why FM stations have numbers like 100.3 on the dial: it's the frequency in MHz.

</details>
