Some atoms don't last forever. This unit tells the story of how that was discovered, shows why a single atom is unpredictable but a crowd of them is not, and puts radiation in perspective: where it comes from, how it helps, and how people stay safe around it. One sim runs through it: [half-life](../../../sims/half-life/index.html), a grid of 400 atoms you can watch decay, or flip a coin for.

**Grades:** 7–8. Lesson 2's coin activity works well from grade 5.

**Before you start:** [Everything is jiggling](../08-heat-and-atoms/01-jiggling-particles.md) and [Ultraviolet and beyond](../04-light-we-cant-see/04-ultraviolet-and-beyond.md).

**Hands on:** 100 coins (or M&Ms with a letter on one side) in a cup. Shake, pour, remove the heads, count, repeat, and graph what's left. You've just modelled half-life.

**Unit check:** if you can explain to someone why you can't predict when one atom will decay but can predict when half of a million will, and why a banana is nothing to worry about, you have this unit at level 4.
