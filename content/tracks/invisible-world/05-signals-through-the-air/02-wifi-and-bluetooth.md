## Start here

Right now, if you're near a router, your room is full of invisible light flashing on and off billions of times a second, carrying videos, messages and games straight through the walls. Why can't you see it? And why does it get worse two rooms away?

## The common wrong idea

"Wifi is some special kind of signal, totally different from light."

Wifi is **microwave light**, the same family as the light from a lamp. Its waves are just much longer: about **12 cm**, roughly the width of your hand. Your eyes can't see waves that long, and luckily they pass harmlessly through you.

## The idea

**What wifi is.** Your router is a radio station and a radio receiver at once. It sends and receives microwaves at about **2.4 or 5 billion wiggles a second** (2.4 GHz and 5 GHz). It changes those waves in patterns to carry bits, and every phone and laptop in the house does the same back.

**How fast it's sending.** A wifi router can send **hundreds of millions of bits every second**. A whole song is about 30 million bits, so a song can arrive in well under a second.

**Why walls and distance matter.**
- The further the signal travels, the more it **spreads out**, like the ripples from a pebble, so less of it reaches your device.
- Walls **soak some of it up**, especially concrete, metal and water. (People are mostly water. A crowded room really does weaken wifi a little.)
- Weaker signal means the noise matters more, so the router slows down and sends more carefully, just like in the sim when the noise goes up.

**Why the microwave oven interferes.** A microwave oven heats food with microwaves at about 2.45 GHz, right next to wifi's 2.4 GHz. A little leaks out (harmlessly, but loudly), and that's noise for your wifi. Using 5 GHz wifi avoids it.

**Bluetooth** uses the same 2.4 GHz band, but whispers instead of shouting. It sends very weak signals so your earbuds' batteries last all day, which is why it only reaches across a room.

## Worked example

Wifi at 2.4 GHz has waves about 12.5 cm long. Light travels 300,000,000 m every second. Check that number.

1. speed = wavelength × frequency, so wavelength = speed ÷ frequency.
2. 00{,}000{,}000 \div 2{,}400{,}000{,}000 = 0.125$ m.
3. That's **12.5 cm**. ✓

## Your turn (faded example)

5 GHz wifi wiggles 5,000,000,000 times a second.

1. wavelength = 300,000,000 ÷ ___
2. wavelength = ___ m = ___ cm

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

00{,}000{,}000 \div 5{,}000{,}000{,}000 = 0.06$ m, which is 6 cm. Shorter waves get soaked up by walls more easily, which is one reason 5 GHz wifi is fast nearby but doesn't reach as far.

</details>

## Practice

1. Your wifi is great in the living room but weak in your bedroom, two walls away. Name two reasons.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The signal has spread out over a longer distance, and each wall soaks some of it up. A router in a more central spot, or a second "mesh" router, helps.

</details>

2. Why do Bluetooth earbuds stop working if you leave your phone in another room?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Bluetooth sends very weak signals to save battery. After a few metres and a wall, too little reaches the earbuds to be read through the noise.

</details>
