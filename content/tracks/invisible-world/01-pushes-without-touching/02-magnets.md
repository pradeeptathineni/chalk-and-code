## Start here

Break a bar magnet in half, right between its north and south ends. Do you get one north piece and one south piece? Guess before you read on.

## The common wrong idea

"North and south are two different stuffs, packed into each end."

Every magnet, however small, has **both** a north and a south pole. Snap a magnet in half and you get two smaller magnets, each with its own north and south. Keep breaking it and the same thing happens, all the way down to single atoms, which behave like tiny magnets themselves.

## The idea

- Every magnet has a **north pole** and a **south pole**.
- **Like poles repel** (north pushes north away). **Unlike poles attract** (north pulls south).
- **Field lines** are a map of the field. They come out of north poles and go into south poles. Where they bunch together, the field is strong. Where they spread out, it's weak.
- A **compass** is a small magnet that can spin. Its north end turns to point along the field lines.
- **The Earth is a giant magnet.** Deep underground, molten iron flowing around makes a magnetic field that wraps the planet. A compass's north end is pulled towards the far north, which is why it works for finding your way. (A fun twist: since the north end of a compass is attracted there, the magnetic pole up in the Arctic is actually a magnetic *south* pole.)

## Worked example

Two bar magnets lie end to end: **S–N** then **N–S**. Do they attract or repel?

1. Find the two ends that face each other: the first magnet's **N** and the second magnet's **N**.
2. Like poles repel.
3. So they **push apart**.

## Your turn (faded example)

Two magnets lie end to end: **N–S** then **N–S**.

1. The ends facing each other are ___ and ___.
2. Like or unlike? ___
3. So they ___.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The facing ends are S (first magnet) and N (second). Unlike poles attract, so they pull together. This is exactly the "Two magnets" setup in the sim.

</details>

## Practice

1. You have a magnet with no labels and a compass. How can you find which end is north?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Bring one end near the compass. The compass's south end is pulled towards a magnet's north pole (unlike poles attract), and its north end is pulled towards a south pole. Whichever end of the compass needle swings to your magnet tells you its pole: if the compass's south end comes over, that end of your magnet is north.

</details>

2. Why do the field lines in the sim never cross each other?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

At any spot, the field points in just one direction. If two lines crossed, the field would point two ways at once at the crossing, which can't happen.

</details>
