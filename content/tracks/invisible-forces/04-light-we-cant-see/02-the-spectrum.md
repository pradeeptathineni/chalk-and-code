> - name the seven kinds of light in order, from the longest waves to the shortest
> - give an everyday use for each one
> - explain which kinds carry more energy and why that matters

## Start here

Your eyes can see red, orange, yellow, green, blue and violet. Guess: out of *all* the light there is, how much can your eyes see? Most of it? Half? A tenth?

## The common wrong idea

"The rainbow is most of the light there is."

The rainbow is a **tiny** slice. Open the sim and find the coloured stripe on the bar: it's barely wider than the marker. Everything else, from waves longer than a football field to waves smaller than an atom, is light our eyes simply can't detect.

## The idea

Put every kind of light in a line, from the longest waves to the shortest, and you get the **electromagnetic spectrum**:

| Kind of light | Wave size, roughly | Where you meet it |
|---|---|---|
| Radio | a person to a sports field, or more | radio, TV, walkie-talkies |
| Microwave | a coin to a ruler | wifi, Bluetooth, phones, GPS, microwave ovens |
| Infrared | a grain of sand, down to a bacterium | TV remotes, heat from warm things, night vision |
| Visible | smaller than a bacterium | everything you see |
| Ultraviolet | a virus | sunburn, black lights |
| X-ray | an atom | X-rays at the doctor and dentist |
| Gamma ray | smaller than an atom | radioactive atoms, exploding stars |

A sentence to remember the order: **R**eal **M**onkeys **I**n **V**ests **U**se **X**-ray **G**oggles.

As the waves get shorter, each little packet of light carries **more energy**. Radio, microwaves, infrared and visible light don't have enough energy to damage the inside of our cells. Ultraviolet has enough to cause sunburn. X-rays and gamma rays have enough to go right through us and damage cells, which is why doctors use them carefully.

## Worked example

Put these in order from longest wave to shortest: **a dentist's X-ray, a TV remote, a microwave oven, a red traffic light**.

1. Find each one's kind of light: X-ray; infrared; microwave; visible.
2. Use the order R M I V U X G: microwave, infrared, visible, X-ray.
3. So: **microwave oven, TV remote, red traffic light, dentist's X-ray**.

## Your turn (faded example)

Put these in order from longest wave to shortest: **sunburn, an FM radio station, your phone's wifi**.

1. Kinds of light: sunburn is ___, FM is ___, wifi is ___.
2. In R M I V U X G order: ___

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Ultraviolet, radio, microwave. Order: **FM radio, wifi, sunburn**.

</details>

## Practice

1. Which carries more energy: infrared or ultraviolet?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Ultraviolet. Its waves are shorter, and shorter waves carry more energy.

</details>

2. Your friend says, "Wifi is radiation, so it must be as dangerous as X-rays." What would you tell them?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are light, and "radiation" just means energy spreading out. But wifi waves are about the size of your hand and each packet of wifi light carries very little energy, even less than a packet of light from a lamp. X-ray waves are about the size of an atom and carry enough energy to damage cells. Same family, very different energy.

</details>
