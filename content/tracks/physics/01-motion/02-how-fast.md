> **After this lesson you can:**
> - calculate average velocity and average speed for a trip
> - read velocity off a position–time graph as its slope
> - explain what a negative velocity means

## Start here

Two friends both finish a 400 m lap of a track in 80 s. One ran steadily; the other sprinted, stopped to tie a shoe, and sprinted again. Did they have the same speed?

## The common wrong idea

"A steeper position graph means the object is higher up" or "is further away."

Height on an $x$–$t$ graph tells you **where** it is. **Steepness** tells you **how fast** the position is changing. Those are different questions.

## The idea

**Average velocity** is displacement divided by time:

$$v_\text{avg} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$$

**Average speed** is distance divided by time. Speed never goes negative; velocity carries a sign for direction.

On a position–time graph, $\frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$ is rise over run. So:

- straight line → constant velocity
- steeper line → faster
- line going down → negative velocity (moving in the negative direction)
- flat line → at rest

## Worked example

A cyclist is at $x = 20\text{ m}$ at $t = 2\text{ s}$ and at $x = 65\text{ m}$ at $t = 11\text{ s}$.

1. $\Delta x = 65 - 20 = 45\text{ m}$
2. $\Delta t = 11 - 2 = 9\text{ s}$
3. $v_\text{avg} = 45 / 9 = 5\text{ m/s}$ in the positive direction.

## Your turn (faded example)

A ball rolls from $x = 12\text{ m}$ at $t = 0$ to $x = 3\text{ m}$ at $t = 3\text{ s}$.

1. $\Delta x = \;?$
2. $v_\text{avg} = \Delta x / \Delta t = \;?$

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$\Delta x = -9\text{ m}$, so $v_\text{avg} = -3\text{ m/s}$: 3 m/s in the negative direction.

</details>

## Practice

1. You drive 30 km east in 0.5 h, then 30 km west in 0.5 h. Find your average speed and your average velocity for the whole hour.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Average speed $= 60\text{ km} / 1\text{ h} = 60\text{ km/h}$. Average velocity $= 0 / 1\text{ h} = 0$, because you ended where you started.

</details>

2. A position graph is a straight line through $(0\text{ s}, 4\text{ m})$ and $(5\text{ s}, -6\text{ m})$. What is the velocity?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

$(-6 - 4)/(5 - 0) = -2\text{ m/s}$.

</details>
