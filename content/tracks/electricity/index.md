Electricity is easy to use and hard to picture. In this track we picture it first, with simulations of charges moving through wires, and then connect every idea to a real part you can hold: a battery, a bulb, an LED, a resistor, a switch, a transistor.

| # | Unit | What it covers | Grades | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is electricity? | charge, current as a flow, conductors and insulators | 3–8 | Planned |
| 2 | Making a circuit | a complete loop, batteries, bulbs and switches; why a broken loop goes dark | 3–6 | Planned |
| 3 | Series and parallel | why some lights all go out together and some don't; how your house is wired | 5–8 | Planned |
| 4 | Voltage, current and resistance | the water-pipe picture, Ohm's law, choosing a resistor for an LED | 6–8 | Planned |
| 5 | Parts with personality | LEDs, motors, buzzers, sensors; reading what a part needs | 6–8 | Planned |
| 6 | The transistor | a switch with no moving parts; using a small current to control a big one | 7–8 | Planned |
| 7 | From switches to computers | how transistors become logic gates, and gates become a computer | 7–8 | Planned |

Unit 7 hands over to [Logic and circuits](../logic-and-circuits/index.md). Unit 2 of [Invisible forces](../invisible-forces/index.md) shows the other side of electricity: magnetism.
