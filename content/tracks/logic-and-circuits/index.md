Every computer is built from a single tiny idea: a switch that is either on or off. In this track middle schoolers go from "true or false?" puzzles to building their own adding machine out of logic gates in Logisim, and then find out that each gate is just a few transistors.

| # | Unit | What it covers | Grades | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | True or false | statements, AND, OR, NOT in plain English; logic puzzles | 4–8 | Planned |
| 2 | Logic gates | the gate symbols, truth tables, light-bulb-and-switch versions of each gate | 5–8 | Planned |
| 3 | Building in Logisim | wiring gates, inputs and outputs, testing every row of a truth table | 6–8 | Planned |
| 4 | Counting in binary | why computers count in 0s and 1s, converting numbers, binary on your fingers | 5–8 | Planned |
| 5 | A machine that adds | half adder, full adder, adding two 4-bit numbers | 6–8 | Planned |
| 6 | Remembering | a latch that holds one bit; how memory starts | 7–8 | Planned |
| 7 | Gates are made of transistors | the transistor as a switch controlled by electricity; building NOT and AND from them | 7–8 | Planned |

Unit 7 is where this track meets [Electricity and electronics](../electricity/index.md): the same transistor that amplifies a signal can also act as the on/off switch inside every gate.
